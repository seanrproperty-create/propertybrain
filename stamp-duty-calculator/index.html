How This Calculator Works

UK stamp duty is a marginal band tax, not a flat percentage of the purchase price — similar to how income tax works. Each slice of the price that falls inside a band is taxed at that band's rate, and only the amount above a threshold moves into the next band up. This calculator applies the published band structures directly: SDLT (Stamp Duty Land Tax) from HMRC for England and Northern Ireland, LBTT (Land and Buildings Transaction Tax) from Revenue Scotland, and LTT (Land Transaction Tax) from the Welsh Revenue Authority.

For additional-property purchases (buy-to-let, second homes, purchases via a limited company), a surcharge is added on top of every band rather than applied as one flat extra percentage — so the calculator recalculates the full banded total with the surcharge baked into each band, not just added to the final figure.

Worked example: A £180,000 additional-property (buy-to-let) purchase in Manchester, England. The price crosses two SDLT bands: the first £125,000 at 5% (0% standard + 5% surcharge), then the remaining £55,000 at 7% (2% standard + 5% surcharge). SDLT due: (£125,000 × 5%) + (£55,000 × 7%) = £6,250 + £3,850 = £10,100 — an effective rate of 5.61% on the full price. Stamp Duty FAQs

PropertyBrain Editorial Team — cross-checked against HMRC, Revenue Scotland and Welsh Revenue Authority guidance. Last verified: 8 August 2026. This is not financial or legal advice — always confirm your exact liability with a solicitor or conveyancer before exchange.
How much stamp duty on a £300,000 additional property?

On a £300,000 additional property in England (2026): £125,000 × 5% (0% standard + 5% surcharge) = £6,250, plus £125,000 × 7% (2% + 5% surcharge) = £8,750, plus £50,000 × 10% (5% + 5% surcharge) = £5,000. Total: £20,000. The effective rate is 6.67%. Use the calculator above to check any price.

What are the current SDLT rates for 2026?

England SDLT 2026: 0% on first £125,000; 2% on £125,001–£250,000; 5% on £250,001–£925,000; 10% on £925,001–£1.5M; 12% above £1.5M. Additional property surcharge: +5% on all bands. First-time buyer relief: 0% up to £300,000, 5% from £300,001–£500,000 (no relief above £500,000).

Is there stamp duty in Scotland?

Scotland uses Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT). Rates: 0% up to £145,000; 2% to £250,000; 5% to £325,000; 10% to £750,000; 12% above. Additional dwellings attract an 8% Additional Dwelling Supplement (ADS, raised from 6% on 5 December 2024) on the full purchase price, added to the banded LBTT.

What is Land Transaction Tax in Wales?

Wales uses Land Transaction Tax (LTT). Standard rates: 0% up to £225,000; 6% to £400,000; 7.5% to £750,000; 10% to £1.5M; 12% above. Since 11 December 2024, additional residential properties no longer pay a flat surcharge on top — they use their own separate higher-rate bands: 5% up to £180,000; 8.5% to £250,000; 10% to £400,000; 12.5% to £750,000; 15% to £1.5M; 17% above.

Do I pay stamp duty on a buy-to-let property?

Yes — and an additional 5% surcharge applies if you already own a residential property. This surcharge applies whether you buy personally or through a limited company. If you're replacing a main residence that was sold, the 5% surcharge may be refunded (within 36 months of purchasing the new home). Always check with a solicitor.

When do I need to pay stamp duty?

You (or usually your solicitor/conveyancer on your behalf) must file an SDLT return and pay any tax due within 14 days of completion. Missing the deadline can trigger penalties and interest from HMRC, so most buyers have their conveyancer handle payment as part of completion.

Do I pay more stamp duty if I'm not a UK resident?

Yes — non-UK residents pay an additional 2% surcharge on top of the standard England/NI rates. This can stack with the 5% additional-property surcharge, so a non-resident buying a second home or investment property could face up to 7 percentage points above the standard rate on the relevant bands. Scotland and Wales have their own separate residency rules — check current guidance before budgeting.

Does stamp duty work differently if I buy through a limited company?

Company purchases of residential property are almost always taxed at the additional-property rates, regardless of whether the company already owns other property. Higher flat rates can also apply to corporate purchases above certain price thresholds. If you're considering buying via a limited company, get specific advice from an accountant or solicitor before you commit — the tax treatment is more complex than a personal purchase.

What if I'm buying more than one property in the same deal?

"Linked transactions" — for example, a small portfolio bought from one seller as part of the same arrangement — have their stamp duty calculated on the combined purchase price, not each property separately. Because SDLT is banded, this usually pushes the deal into higher bands than if the properties were bought in separate, unrelated transactions. Specialist relief may apply for six or more residential properties bought in one transaction — check with a solicitor.
